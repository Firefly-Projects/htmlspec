Record a renamed requirement

This change made the contract let a requirement state the heading it replaced, in a renamed-from paragraph kept separate from its change state (A renamed requirement states the heading it replaced), and made a merged rename reach the accepted contract as a new heading on the same identifier (A rename reaches the accepted contract as a new heading on the same identifier). It made sync and archival compare a renamed requirement's heading name with the accepted one and report a rename the accepted specifications have not taken (The command surface reports whether a change reached the accepted contract, Archival assesses the merge and never performs it). It made migration carry a source rename as the heading the requirement replaced, under the one identifier a chain of renames keeps (A source rename is carried as the heading the requirement replaced, Identifiers are derived once and every reference resolves through that derivation).

document-schema

A renamed requirement states the heading it replaced req-a-renamed-requirement-states-the-heading-it-replaced

A requirement unit SHALL be able to state the heading it replaced, in one paragraph marked data-field="renamed-from" whose text is that previous heading. The requirement's own heading SHALL be its current name. The pair is OpenSpec's FROM:/TO: rename, expressed on the unit.

A rename SHALL be stated separately from the unit's change state. The change state SHALL describe what happened to the requirement's statements and scenarios, and the renamed-from paragraph SHALL describe what happened to its heading. A requirement that is renamed and rewritten in one change SHALL carry data-change="modified" and a renamed-from paragraph. A requirement that is renamed and otherwise untouched SHALL carry data-change="unchanged" and a renamed-from paragraph. Neither fact SHALL be inferred from the other.

The reason is that a rename and a modification are different facts, and a change can make either without the other. A single value that meant "renamed" would force a requirement that was renamed and rewritten to give up one of the two, and both of this project's own renames are that case.

A renamed requirement SHALL keep its identifier, as "Every semantic unit has a stable unique identifier" already requires. The renamed-from paragraph is what lets a reader who knows the previous heading find the requirement under its new one.

A renamed-from paragraph SHALL NOT supply the requirement's normative statement, and its text SHALL project into the requirement's renamedFrom field rather than into its statements.

The following SHALL make a document invalid:

  • a renamed-from paragraph in any unit other than a requirement;

  • a renamed-from paragraph in a requirement whose change state is added, because a requirement this change introduces had no previous heading;

  • a renamed-from paragraph in a requirement whose change state is removed, because a withdrawn requirement is not also renamed, and OpenSpec rejects a rename and a removal of one requirement;

  • more than one renamed-from paragraph in one requirement;

  • a renamed-from paragraph whose text is empty after whitespace normalization;

  • a renamed-from paragraph whose text equals the requirement's own heading after whitespace normalization, because a rename to the same name renames nothing.

A requirement's heading, for every comparison a rename makes, SHALL be its heading name: its projected title with the requirement's own identifier removed from the end, then trimmed. A heading commonly shows its identifier beside the name (<span class="hs-id">req-x</span>), and the projected title carries that text. A previous heading is stated as the name a reader read, without the identifier. So a comparison with the title itself would never find the two equal.

artifact-kinds

A rename reaches the accepted contract as a new heading on the same identifier req-a-rename-reaches-the-accepted-contract-as-a-new-heading-on-the-same-identifier

Where a change's specification states that a requirement replaced a previous heading, applying that specification's contract to the project's accepted specifications SHALL give the accepted requirement carrying that identifier the new heading, and SHALL NOT change its identifier.

The accepted specification SHALL NOT carry the renamed-from paragraph. The accepted contract states what the project requires now, under the name it uses now, and the history of a heading belongs to the archived change that renamed it. This is the arrangement OpenSpec keeps: a main specification holds no RENAMED section, and the rename survives in the change that made it.

Where the change also modified the requirement, the merge SHALL apply the modification to the same accepted requirement. The rename and the modification SHALL be applied as two facts about one requirement, and neither SHALL be applied as a removal of the old requirement followed by the addition of a new one, because that would regenerate an identifier the schema requires to be stable.

Where the accepted requirement carrying that identifier already has the new heading, applying the rename SHALL change nothing. A rename applied before archival, by the operation that applies a change's contract, is the ordinary case, and archival SHALL NOT treat it as a conflict.

The merge remains the agent's work under "Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven". This requirement states what a correctly applied rename leaves behind, not a program that applies it.

toolchain-packaging

Archival assesses the merge and never performs it req-archival-assesses-the-merge-and-never-performs-it

Archival SHALL determine whether the change's contract reached the accepted specifications, and SHALL do so by comparing requirement identifiers, and for a renamed requirement its heading, rather than by reading requirements for meaning. This SHALL be the comparison the reporting operation makes, derived from one source, including its treatment of a rename the accepted specifications have not taken.

Archival SHALL NOT edit an accepted specification, SHALL NOT propose the text of a merge, and SHALL NOT treat an absent identifier as an error. Whether an absent requirement was withdrawn deliberately or omitted by mistake is a judgment, and artifact-kinds reserves it for an agent.

Where the assessment finds the contract has not reached the accepted specifications, archival SHALL report that the change has not been synced and SHALL name the operation that reports what remains. The report SHALL name the operation as it is invoked, rather than describing it. The prior wording told a contributor to apply the change's contract at a time when no operation did so, and a report that names a step without naming the command behind it leaves the reader searching the command surface for a name they must guess.

Where archival asks whether to proceed over its findings, and the change has not been synced, the question SHALL say so and SHALL state that declining leaves the project unchanged. A question that asks only whether to archive anyway invites the answer that archives a change whose contract never reached the project's specifications; a contributor deciding that should be told what proceeding skips and that declining costs them nothing.

The structured payload SHALL state whether the change has not been synced, as a value rather than as something derivable from the reported identifiers. An agent driving archival cannot answer the question a contributor is asked, so the condition that question is about SHALL be readable without inference.

Archival SHALL NOT perform the merge it names, and the report SHALL NOT be taken as authority to. This is the same prohibition stated above, restated because naming an operation is where a reader would expect an offer to run it.

Where the assessment finds the contract has reached the accepted specifications, archival SHALL NOT report anything about syncing. A report that appears whether or not it applies is one a reader learns to pass over, and the occasion it mattered would be passed over with it.

The command surface reports whether a change reached the accepted contract req-the-command-surface-reports-whether-a-change-reached-the-accepted-contract

The command surface SHALL provide an operation that reports, for one change, whether the requirements its specification states have reached the project's accepted specifications. The report SHALL be available as a structured payload.

The operation SHALL determine this by comparing requirement identifiers, and, for a renamed requirement, headings, and SHALL read neither the change's specification nor the accepted specifications for meaning. This is the same comparison archival makes, and both SHALL derive it from one source, so that a contributor who runs the operation and the archival that follows cannot disagree about which requirements reached the contract.

The report SHALL distinguish three sets: the requirement identifiers the change states, those the accepted specifications already hold, and those absent from them. The third is derivable from the first two and SHALL be reported anyway, because it is the answer the operation exists to give.

A renamed requirement SHALL be compared by its heading as well as its identifier. A rename keeps the requirement's identifier, so a comparison of identifiers alone reports a rename the accepted specifications never took as reached. Where a requirement the change states carries a renamed-from paragraph, and the accepted specifications hold its identifier, the operation SHALL compare that requirement's heading with the heading of the accepted requirement carrying the same identifier. Where they differ, the requirement SHALL be reported in a fourth set: renames the accepted specifications have not taken. The comparison SHALL be of the two heading names — each heading without the identifier shown beside it, as document-schema's "A renamed requirement states the heading it replaced" defines — after whitespace normalization, which is an exact comparison and not a reading for meaning.

A renamed requirement whose identifier the accepted specifications do not hold SHALL be reported as absent, as any other requirement is, and SHALL NOT also be reported as an untaken rename. A requirement in the fourth set SHALL be counted as not having reached the contract, so a change whose only unapplied work is a rename is reported as not synced.

The operation SHALL NOT modify an accepted specification, and SHALL NOT propose the text of a merge. Deciding which accepted requirement a scenario belongs under is judgment, and artifact-kinds reserves it for an agent. This prohibition is stated rather than left implied: the operation carries OpenSpec's name for it, that name reads like an operation that writes, and a reader who assumes it does would be assuming the program edits the project's contract unattended.

An absent requirement SHALL be a finding and not an error, and the operation SHALL succeed while any remain. A change mid-implementation legitimately states requirements that have not reached the contract, and a requirement withdrawn deliberately is absent permanently; an operation that failed on either would fail on the normal state of the work it is meant to be run during.

The operation SHALL refuse on a change that cannot be resolved, on an absent specification artifact, and on a specification that does not validate. A comparison made against an invalid contract has no meaning, and reporting it as though it did is worse than declining to report.

project-migration

A source rename is carried as the heading the requirement replaced req-a-source-rename-is-carried-as-the-heading-the-requirement-replaced

Where a source delta carries a ## RENAMED Requirements section, migration SHALL carry each FROM:/TO: pair into the migrated change's specification as the renamed requirement's renamed-from paragraph, stating the FROM: heading, on the requirement whose heading is the TO: heading.

The requirement's change state SHALL be taken from the rest of the delta and not from the rename. Where the same delta also restates the requirement under its MODIFIED Requirements section — which is how OpenSpec records a rename and a rewrite together, the MODIFIED block naming the new heading — the requirement SHALL carry data-change="modified". Where the delta renames the requirement and says nothing else about it, the requirement SHALL carry data-change="unchanged", and its statements and scenarios SHALL be those the requirement held before the rename.

Any prose the source's RENAMED section carried beside its pairs — an explanation of why the heading changed — SHALL be carried into the migrated specification rather than dropped, because it is the author's reason for the rename and no field holds it.

A rename SHALL NOT be recorded as an open question once the destination can express it. The requirement "A source distinction the destination cannot express is recorded as open, not mapped to the nearest state" governs distinctions the destination has no value for, and a rename is no longer one.

A source distinction the destination cannot express is recorded as open, not mapped to the nearest state req-a-source-distinction-the-destination-cannot-express-is-recorded-as-open-not-mapped-to-the-nearest-state

Where a source entity carries a distinction the destination's contract has no value for, migration SHALL record it as an open question and SHALL name the change that would settle it. Migration SHALL NOT map the distinction onto the nearest available value.

The nearest available value is the tempting choice because it validates. A distinction mapped onto it is indistinguishable, afterwards, from a source that never made the distinction — so the information is not merely lost but unrecoverable, and no check can report it because the result conforms.

Settling the question instead SHALL be out of scope for a migration. Adding a value to the destination's vocabulary reaches validation, formatting, and presentation, and a migration decides it in bulk inside a change whose subject is something else. This is the same reason migration does not invent a layout where the contract is silent.

The plan SHALL record, for each such entity, what the source stated, that the destination has no value for it, and what migration did instead.

Identifiers are derived once and every reference resolves through that derivation req-identifiers-are-derived-once-and-every-reference-resolves-through-that-derivation

Migration SHALL derive each requirement's identifier once, and every reference to that requirement anywhere in the migrated project SHALL use the derived identifier. The derivation SHALL be recorded in the plan.

Deriving independently at each site produces different identifiers for one obligation, each locally valid, so nothing reports the divergence and cross-references silently fail to resolve.

The derivation SHALL be recorded against the source document that stated the heading, rather than against the heading alone. A source project can state one continuing obligation under different headings in different changes, because a change may rename a requirement, and each change's specification states what that change established rather than what the project later called it. A derivation keyed on the heading alone would either merge those statements — misrepresenting every change but the last — or report collisions where the source is consistent.

Where the source records a rename, the renamed requirement SHALL keep the identifier it already held. A requirement named in a source ## RENAMED Requirements section is one obligation under a new heading, and document-schema's "Every semantic unit has a stable unique identifier" requires a renamed requirement to keep its identifier. So its identifier SHALL be the one derived from the heading under which the requirement was first established, and a chain of renames SHALL carry that one identifier through every change in the chain and into the accepted specification. The plan SHALL record, against the document that stated each rename, the heading it replaced, the heading it introduced, and the identifier both resolve to.

Where the source changed a heading without recording a rename — by removing one requirement and adding another — the two SHALL derive their identifiers independently. The source did not state that they are one obligation, and migration SHALL NOT infer it from their wording.

The derivation SHALL read the heading in full. A derivation that shortens or normalizes a heading maps distinct obligations onto one identifier, which manufactures a collision the source did not contain and refers the contributor to a conflict that is an artifact of the rule.

Where two source requirements in one destination document derive the same identifier, migration SHALL report the collision rather than resolving it by suffixing. Two distinct obligations distinguished only by a trailing digit read as versions of one, which is a worse outcome than a migration that stops and asks. Collapsing several of a source change's specifications into one document SHALL be understood as the operation most likely to produce such a collision, because it is the one that brings requirements from different parts of the source into a single identifier namespace.
