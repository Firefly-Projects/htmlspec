Resolve a reference across its change

Under document-schema's "Cross-references address units by identifier", a reference written in data-refs resolves beyond the document that makes it, by where that document sits: a change's proposal, design, and tasks reach that change's specification, every one of a change's documents reaches the accepted specifications, an accepted specification reaches the others, and the first place that declares the identifier decides which unit the reference means, a withdrawn one included. Under document-validation's "Cross-references must resolve", validation reads the documents a reference reaches only for the identifiers they declare, and reports a reference that resolves nowhere with the places it searched and any document it could not read, and toolchain-packaging's "The command surface names its operations as OpenSpec names them" has validating one document report on that document alone. Under "The contract rules apply to every kind", a document's kind does not decide what its references reach, and runbook-execution's "A record carries the procedure it ran and the obligations it attests to" gives a runbook's documents, which reach only themselves, as the reason a record copies its requirements.

document-schema

Cross-references address units by identifier req-cross-references-address-units-by-identifier

A task, verification, evidence, or risk unit MAY reference the semantic units it relates to. Such a reference SHALL name the target by its identifier, and SHALL resolve to a unit that exists in the same document or in an accepted speca document reachable from it.

A reference written in a data-refs attribute SHALL be resolved in the following places, in this order, and SHALL resolve to the unit declared in the first place that declares its identifier:

  1. the document that makes the reference;

  2. where that document is one of a change's documents other than its specification, that change's own specification;

  3. where that document is one of a change's documents, including its specification, or is an accepted specification, the project's accepted specifications, every one of them.

A change's documents are the documents directly within a change's directory, whether the change is active or archived. The project's accepted specifications are the documents beneath the place where the project keeps them. Nothing else is reachable. A document outside any project, a file beneath a change's evidence, and a runbook's documents reach only themselves. A change's documents do not reach another change's documents, and they do not reach their own change's proposal, design, or tasks.

A change's other documents reach its specification because they exist to serve it. A proposal argues for what the specification states, a design reasons about it, and the tasks carry it out. The tasks template already tells an author that a task may reference the requirement it serves "once that requirement exists in this change's specification". The accepted specifications are reachable because they are the contract every change starts from.

The order decides which unit a reference means when two places declare one identifier. A change's specification restates, under the accepted identifier, each accepted requirement the change modifies or withdraws. For the change's own documents, the change's statement is the one that counts. So a task that references a requirement its change withdraws targets a withdrawn obligation, even while the accepted specification still holds that requirement unchanged.

Every accepted specification is reachable, and an accepted specification reaches the others. A reference names a unit, not a capability. The merge that applies a change places each requirement in the accepted specification for its capability, so a reference that resolved in a change's specification must still resolve after that change's units are merged into two different accepted specifications.

A reference written as a fragment link, href="#…", SHALL resolve within the document that makes it. It is a hyperlink into that page. If it resolved somewhere else while the link arrived nowhere, the reference would be true and the hyperlink false.

Where more than one accepted specification declares an identifier, a reference to it SHALL resolve. Which of them it means is not decided here.

A reference SHALL NOT resolve to a unit that the place resolving it marks removed. This holds in every place, not only in the document that makes the reference. A task and the requirement it serves rarely share a document, so a rule confined to one document would miss the case the scenario "Reference to a removed requirement" names.

document-validation

Cross-references must resolve req-cross-references-must-resolve

Validation SHALL verify that every reference to a semantic unit resolves to a unit that exists. This includes fragment links within the document, and references from tasks, verification, evidence, and risks to the requirements and scenarios they address.

A reference resolves in the places that document-schema's "Cross-references address units by identifier" makes reachable. Validation SHALL read the other documents a reference can reach for the identifiers they declare, and SHALL report nothing about them. A diagnostic belongs to the document being validated. The documents it reaches are reported when they are validated themselves, so a defect in one of them is reported once, against the document that has it.

A reference that resolves nowhere SHALL be reported against the unit that makes it, and the report SHALL name the places that were searched. "No unit in the document declares that identifier" is true of a document that reaches only itself. For a task in a change it would hide that the change's specification and the accepted specifications were searched too.

A reachable document that cannot be read, or whose markup needs the parser to recover, declares nothing a reference can rely on. A reference that resolves in no other place SHALL be reported, and the report SHALL name that document as unread. It SHALL NOT claim that no unit declares the identifier. A document with more than one reading has no determinate content, so identifiers taken from it are a guess. And a report that no unit declares the identifier would send the author to add a unit that may already be there.

A change that has no specification yet is a change in progress. Its specification is then a place that declares nothing, and it SHALL NOT be reported as a document that could not be read.

The contract rules apply to every kind req-the-contract-rules-apply-to-every-kind

The rules that make a document readable, parseable, and offline SHALL apply to all four kinds without relaxation. Freedom of structure at the proposal and design kinds SHALL NOT extend to markup that cannot be parsed unambiguously, a reference that resolves to nothing, a remote or executable dependency, or an asset reference that disagrees with the bytes beside it.

A kind decides how much shape validation requires. It SHALL NOT decide whether a document opens.

A kind SHALL NOT decide what a reference can reach, either. That is decided by where a document sits, so a change's proposal, design, and tasks reach the same documents.

runbook-execution

A record carries the procedure it ran and the obligations it attests to req-a-record-carries-the-procedure-it-ran-and-the-obligations-it-attests-to

A run's record SHALL carry the runbook's procedure, copied, with each step's state advanced as the run advanced it. A record that referenced the procedure instead of carrying it could not say which version of the procedure ran, which is the question the record exists to answer: a procedure improves, and a record naming one that has since changed describes a run that did not happen that way.

A run's record SHALL carry the requirements it attests to, copied from the runbook's specification, each with the scenarios that make it checkable. This is forced rather than chosen. A reference from a runbook's documents addresses a unit within one document, sothe document that makes it: document-schema's "Cross-references address units by identifier" lets a reference reach beyond its own document only from a change's documents or an accepted specification, and a runbook is neither. So a record naming a requirement in the runbook's specification resolves to nothing and is reported as an error — a record that pointed outward would be invalid on the day it was written.

Each copied requirement SHALL state its obligation in a form that carries an obligation, and SHALL declare itself unchanged. Both follow from the copy being a faithful one: a requirement whose modal is weakened states a preference, and a requirement declared as being added claims an obligation that is not there yet.

A run's record SHALL record, for each requirement it carries, an outcome and how that outcome was reached, precisely enough that someone else could repeat it. The outcome and the method are the recorded values, and a record stating an outcome with no method is a claim nobody can check.

The copy SHALL be whole at the moment the run starts rather than grown as the run proceeds. A partial copy presents itself as a complete procedure — nothing in a list of steps says there are more — so a reader between two steps would see a version of the procedure that never existed.

A run's record SHALL be of the artifact kind that carries work, and the set of kinds SHALL NOT gain one for a run. A record is a copy of a procedure with its states advanced, which is what that kind already is; and the kind that carries obligations refuses a step outright, so a record declared as one would be invalid for every step it carried.

toolchain-packaging

The command surface names its operations as OpenSpec names them req-a-single-command-exposes-validate-format-and-init

The project SHALL provide one command-line program offering, at minimum, a subcommand to validate documents, a subcommand to format documents, a subcommand to bootstrap a project, and a subcommand to create documents. Each subcommand SHALL be usable without configuration in a conforming project.

Where an operation corresponds to one OpenSpec provides, it SHALL carry OpenSpec's name for it. This holds for the subcommand and for the options that select what it acts on. Every plausible adopter arrives from OpenSpec — HTMLSpec is a spinoff and its README says so — and a contributor who has learned one surface SHALL NOT have to learn a second spelling for the same operation. An operation OpenSpec does not provide takes the name this project chooses for it.

The spellings are therefore fixed rather than left to an implementer: validate, archive, init, new, list, status, sync, instructions, and store name the operations OpenSpec also provides. format and assets name operations it does not. The selectors --json, --specs, --change, and --store carry OpenSpec's spellings for the same reason.

Fixing a spelling in the contract is not decoration. A requirement that describes an operation by role alone — an operation that enumerates a project's active changes — is satisfied by a subcommand called changes, or ls, and the instructions written against it would then name a command that does not exist. The workflow instructions this project installs invoke these commands by name, so the name is part of the contract they depend on.

The bootstrap subcommand's subject SHALL be a project and it SHALL require no arguments, because the operation a contributor needs first is the one that establishes the project they are standing in. The creation subcommand's subject SHALL be a document or a change.

These SHALL be distinct subcommands. One subcommand meaning both "establish this project" and "write this file" would leave a contributor unable to ask for either without also specifying which they meant.

Creation SHALL take the artifact kind of the document to create, and SHALL produce a document already shaped for that kind. A proposal SHALL be initialized as a page of prose with no unit scaffolding at all, and a specification SHALL be initialized with the units its kind expects.

The asymmetry is the requirement. A proposal that arrived full of empty requirement scaffolding would push its author toward stating a contract at the stage meant for arguing about one, and the emptiest possible starting point is the correct one for a document whose structure is the author's to choose.

Creation SHALL be able to create a whole change — the directory and its documents — rather than one document at a time, because a change is a directory of kinded documents. A change created in a project SHALL be placed where that project keeps its active changes, and the resolved location SHALL be reported.

Documents SHALL be created referencing the project's asset store rather than establishing one of their own.
