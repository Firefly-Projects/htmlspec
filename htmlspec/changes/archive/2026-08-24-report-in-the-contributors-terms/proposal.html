Why

A contributor described a tally-book app, got three good documents, and read a report that told them none of it. Their reaction:

If I was a user I asked to make a tally book app and I got a bunch of nothing back.

The report they received led with a task order, closed on a validation warning count, and asked them to adjudicate a collapse threshold of 50 and a reset-per-box behavior they had never mentioned. The documents themselves were good — the proposal states the need as an absence, names who wants it, and describes counting three bird species on a walk.

Two faults, and they compound.

Every report obligation names an artifact. new.md closes with five bullets:

  • Where the change landed, and the three documents it holds.
  • What the proposal argues, and what it excludes.
  • The decisions the design reached, and any alternative you rejected for a reason worth keeping.
  • The task order, and why the first task is first.
  • Anything you could not settle.

The subject of each one is a directory, an argument, decisions, an order, and open questions. Not one names the thing the contributor asked about. An agent that follows all five produces exactly the report received, so that report is obedient rather than careless.

This is not new's defect. All seven bodies carry a ## What to report section, and every one is artifact-subjected. Counting lines in each that name an artifact, a document, a task, a design, a specification, or a warning:

Body Artifact-subjected lines
apply.md 6
new.md 5
verify.md 5
archive.md 4
ff.md 4
continue.md 3
explore.md 2

apply.md is worse than new.md. verify.md asks for a per-task and per-decision accounting, which is a pointer list by construction.

Pointers arrive unglossed, and a report is where that costs most. The received report listed ten task identifiers as a numbered sequence. One (task-test-harness) was named; the rest appeared as bare numerals, and the numbering restarted so that items 6 through 10 were referenced but never shown. Reading it required opening tasks.html and cross-referencing ten identifiers.

Applying the deletion test that this project's own explainer standard already uses — remove every pointer and see whether the sentence still states its claim — the list collapses to nothing. The pointers are the claim, so each one owes a plain-words gloss. No body asks for one: grep -ril "gloss\|plain words" across all seven instruction files returns nothing.

Why a report rather than a document. A report is the only part of a workflow a contributor is guaranteed to read. The documents serve the next agent and archival, and a contributor opens them by choice. So the report carries the whole burden of answering one question: did this understand me? Five bullets about artifacts cannot answer it, however faithfully an agent follows them.

What Changes

  • A report speaks in the contributor's terms. The first thing a report says is stated in the vocabulary of what the contributor asked for, rather than the vocabulary of the artifacts that hold it. Each workflow keeps its own subject — explore reports on a question, verify on whether the change is sound, archive on what the project now guarantees — and the register is shared.

  • Every pointer is glossed on first use. A task identifier, a rule number, a diagnostic code, or a warning count names in plain words what it points at, in the same sentence, or it does not appear in a report. The deletion test decides the case: where removing the pointers leaves the claim standing, they annotate a stated fact and no gloss is owed.

  • A report surfaces prose the workflow already wrote. The tally proposal's What working looks like section — "Click Robin seven times. It reads 7, drawn as one struck group of five followed by two upright marks" — is the answer to "what am I getting", and it sat unread in a file while the report discussed a threshold. Where an artifact already states the thing in the contributor's terms, the report quotes or compresses it rather than describing the file.

  • A decision the agent can settle is settled, not delegated. A collapse threshold of 50 is arbitrary and cheap to change, so an agent picks one and says so. Scope the contributor did not ask for is offered for removal rather than presented as an open question. What remains for the contributor is what genuinely needs them.

  • The obligations live in the contract, once. A new workflow-reporting capability states them for every workflow. A rule stated seven times in seven bodies drifts seven ways, and the existing divergence across those seven report sections is the evidence.

Non-Goals

  • Making a workflow do more work. new writes three documents and no code, by design, and that is correct. If a contributor expected running code, the repair is a report that names the boundary — "three documents exist, the app does not yet, apply builds it" — not a workflow that quietly starts building.

  • Removing the artifact facts. Where the change landed and what the checks reported are real and a contributor sometimes needs them. They move from the opening to the end and from the subject to the annotation. Nothing is deleted.

  • Rewriting any body's procedure. This change reaches the ## What to report section of each body and the contract that governs it. The guidance on how to write a proposal, a design, or a specification is untouched.

  • A fixed report template. Seven workflows have seven subjects, and a shape that suits new would distort explore, which deliberately produces no artifact at all. The obligation is on register and on glossing, not on a form to fill in.

  • Importing the explainer's full language standard. The deletion test and the pointer-gloss rule transfer because a report has a reader with limited time. Sentence caps, the modal ladder, and the rest of that standard are for a different kind of document and are not adopted here.

  • Re-opening pass-the-description-through or accept-a-described-change. Both are archived and both work. This defect was visible only once a description reached an agent and the agent produced a full report, which is why it surfaces now rather than earlier.
