Keep archived documents pointing at their store

This change makes archival keep every relative reference in a change's documents reaching what it reached before the move, inside its transaction, and makes validation resolve an asset reference only where its path reaches the store, not wherever its address is stored.

toolchain-packaging

Archival produces the specification and updates the accepted contract req-archival-produces-the-specification-and-updates-the-accepted-contract

The command surface SHALL provide an archival operation that produces a change's specification artifact from its proposal, design, and tasks, validates it, and then applies its contract changes to the project's accepted specifications.

There SHALL be one specification artifact per change, and it SHALL be the same file as that change's as-built record. The project's accepted specifications SHALL be a separate destination outside the change directory.

The production SHALL be performed by an agent following stated instructions rather than by a deterministic extractor in the program. The program's part SHALL be the checking: it validates the produced document and reports what is wrong with it. That division is deliberate — the judgment of what a design's prose obliges is not derivable by rule, while whether the result conforms is exactly what a validator determines.

The project SHALL NOT ship a command that parses a proposal or a design and emits requirements. Those kinds carry no imposed structure, so there is nothing for such a command to parse reliably, and one written anyway would produce a specification that passes every check and misstates what the change established.

Archival SHALL NOT complete while the produced specification reports an error. A change archived with an invalid specification would leave the accepted contract in a state the project's own tools reject.

The archival command SHALL complete the operation it is named for. On success the change directory SHALL have been moved into the project's archive, and the change SHALL have recorded the version the project declared. An archival that reports success while the change remains among the project's active changes SHALL NOT be considered to have archived it.

Archival SHALL be atomic with respect to the project. Where archival refuses, or where a contributor declines to proceed, the project SHALL be left exactly as archival found it: no change moved, no accepted specification altered, and no version recorded.

Archival SHALL keep every relative reference in the change's documents reaching the file it reached before the move. A reference that would reach a different file from the archive SHALL be rewritten to reach the same one. A reference within the change, a URL, an absolute path, a bare fragment, and an author's quotation of a reference in text SHALL be left as written, and the change's evidence SHALL NOT be rewritten.

The rewrite SHALL be part of archival's transaction. A refusal, or an interruption before the change moves, SHALL leave every document as it was, and an interruption after the move SHALL be completed by the next archival.

document-validation

An asset reference must match the bytes it names req-an-asset-reference-must-match-the-bytes-it-names

Validation SHALL reject a document whose asset reference does not resolve to stored bytes whose hash equals the hash named by that reference. A reference naming bytes the project does not store SHALL be reported as unresolved. A reference resolving to bytes whose hash differs from the name SHALL be reported as a mismatch, and the two SHALL be distinguishable diagnostics because they have different causes.

A reference SHALL resolve only where its path, followed from the document's directory, reaches the directory of the store above the document. A reference whose path reaches another directory SHALL be reported as unresolved, whatever bytes its address names, and the report SHALL name the directory the path leads to and the path to the store.

This rule SHALL apply only to a document validated within a project that has an asset store. A document validated outside such a project SHALL NOT have its asset references resolved, and SHALL NOT be rejected for naming bytes that no store holds. The rule states a relationship between a document and the bytes beside it, and where there are no bytes there is no relationship to violate.

Where a document's asset reference ascends out of the document's own directory, and no asset store is found in any directory above it, validation SHALL report that reference. Such a reference asserts that a store sits above the document, and the absence of one contradicts the reference rather than placing the document outside the rule's scope.

A reference that does not ascend SHALL remain unreported when no store is found. This is what keeps a document whose references are symbolic — a conformance fixture, or a document written to be read rather than rendered — outside the rule, and the distinction is exact: a fixture's references name a store beside the document that has never existed, while an ascending reference names a store the document was generated against.

A document SHALL NOT be able to exempt itself. The scope SHALL be determined by whether a store is present and by the shape of the reference, never by a declaration inside the document, because a rule a document can switch off protects nothing. Reference shape is not such a declaration: it is written by whatever generated the document, and changing it to silence the rule breaks the reference it describes.

This rule SHALL be an error rather than a warning, and SHALL NOT be reducible to a warning. A document that renders against bytes other than the ones it names is misreporting its own provenance.
