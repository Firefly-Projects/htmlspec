runbook-portability

Machine requirements are explicit and mechanically checkable req-machine-requirements-are-explicit-and-mechanically-checkable

A runbook SHALL state machine prerequisites as ordinary requirement units in its specification, with stable identifiers, normative statements, scenarios, and an explicit check declaration. Preflight SHALL read these declarations from the existing projected requirement notes. It SHALL NOT infer checks from prose, requirement names, or executable examples. The declaration format SHALL be documented and restricted to built-in checks; arbitrary commands SHALL NOT be accepted.

The supported needs SHALL include a tool at a stated version, a tool supplied by a pinned package-manager environment, an installed agent skill, and a working-directory state. Machine requirements SHALL remain part of the logging contract: passing preflight does not remove their obligation to have an outcome recorded. Ordinary requirements without a check declaration SHALL remain logging obligations without becoming machine checks.

Malformed declarations, unknown check kinds, duplicate declarations on one requirement, and unsupported format versions SHALL be reported against the requirement and SHALL prevent starting a run. A contract with no machine declarations SHALL report zero checks, without claiming to have established unstated prerequisites.

A second declaration format, preflight/v2, SHALL read every form preflight/v1 reads and one optional field, remedy: text saying where to get what the check needs. A remedy SHALL be one line of 1 to 200 characters once the spaces around it are trimmed, holding no control character, no whitespace but the plain space, and no invisible character, Unicode's format and default-ignorable characters, beyond the two joiners and one emoji presentation selector directly after the character it is for. A remedy breaking any of these SHALL make the declaration malformed. A remedy is text: preflight SHALL NOT run it, open an address in it, or count it as satisfying the check. preflight/v1 SHALL keep refusing the field, and an unsupported format version SHALL be reported naming the formats the program reads.

A third declaration format, preflight/v3, SHALL read every form preflight/v2 reads, with its remedy, and one more kind, run, whose fields are runbook and an optional atLeast, and whose need is a finished, passed run of that runbook recorded in the same project. preflight/v1 and preflight/v2 SHALL refuse run as an unknown kind, and the fault SHALL say it is declared in preflight/v3. A run check that names its own runbook, and one whose run checks lead back to its own runbook through others, SHALL be malformed, and SHALL prevent starting a run as every malformed declaration does, naming the cycle in order.

A prerequisite run is met by a finished, passed record req-a-prerequisite-run-is-met-by-a-finished-passed-record

A run check SHALL name its runbook by a runbook directory whose name equals the declared name byte for byte, whatever the filesystem. A name that no runbook directory holds SHALL leave the check unmet, with a remedy that says to add the runbook or correct the name.

A record under that runbook's runs/ folder SHALL satisfy the check when it parses without error recovery, is a tasks document, has at least one task done and every task done or dropped, and records an outcome of passed for every requirement it carries. Any satisfying record SHALL meet the check, because a record states no time and no record is the latest. A record that is a symbolic link, or resolves outside that runbook's runs/ folder, SHALL NOT satisfy it, because the run it records may have happened in another project.

Where atLeast is declared, a record SHALL satisfy the check only when the runbook release it states is at or above atLeast within the same major. A record's release SHALL be read from the one htmlspec-runbook-release meta in its head and from nowhere else, and a record that states none, states one that is not three dot-separated integers, or states more than one SHALL NOT satisfy a check with atLeast.

A record that does not satisfy the check SHALL be named with the first reason it does not, in this order: it is a link, it cannot be read, it needs error recovery, it is not a tasks document, it has no task done, it is still open, it recorded an outcome other than passed, it records no outcome for a requirement, or its release is missing, malformed, stated twice, or misses atLeast. Up to three satisfying records SHALL be named, with a count of the rest, and every run of the named runbook still open SHALL be named whether the check is met or not.

A specification reached through the run checks that cannot be read, needs error recovery, or does not validate SHALL leave the check unavailable, naming that runbook, and never met.

Preflight gates the creation of a run req-preflight-gates-the-creation-of-a-run

Starting a run SHALL perform preflight against the selected runbook and the invocation's current working directory and environment before creating the record or its directory. A standalone preflight success SHALL NOT be reusable as permission to skip this check. Only a result in which every declared machine requirement passes SHALL permit record creation; failed, unknown, timed-out, and unsupported results SHALL refuse it. There SHALL be no force or skip-preflight option.

Preflight SHALL execute no procedure steps and SHALL not modify the runbook, existing records, skills, or target working tree. Checks SHALL have bounded execution and captured output. An interrupted preflight SHALL stop its probes and publish no record. Each retry SHALL collect fresh observations. Readiness describes observations at the beginning of a run, not a guarantee about the environment throughout the procedure.

A successfully created record SHALL carry the runbook version and a verification of each passed machine requirement, with its observation and method. Other logging requirements SHALL remain unverified until the run records their outcomes. Checking or editing an existing record SHALL NOT become subject to preflight; run conformance remains advisory as established by log-a-runbook-run.

A run check SHALL read only the records of the runbook it names, in the project the declaring runbook is read from, the store's under --store, and SHALL read nothing else. An unmet run check SHALL refuse the start as an unmet machine requirement does, before anything is written, and no option SHALL override it. A new record SHALL state the release of the runbook it ran in an htmlspec-runbook-release meta in its head, and the verification it carries for a passed run check SHALL name up to three satisfying records with their releases and a count of the rest.

A carried runbook does not require its source project's history req-a-carried-runbook-does-not-require-its-source-project-s-history

A carried runbook SHALL be usable with its specification, procedure, version, changelog, and the referenced shared asset store in a minimal receiving project layout. It SHALL NOT require the source project's archive, project declaration, accepted specifications, or old run records to perform preflight or start a new local run. The receiving layout SHALL preserve one asset store shared by the runbooks and their records; it SHALL NOT introduce a store inside each runbook.

The runbook SHALL require tools and skills from the receiving machine rather than vendor them. Its dependency manifests SHALL be supplied by the target work environment when required. Old run records SHALL remain at their source unless separately copied; their absence at the destination SHALL NOT imply they never existed or prevent a new run. Transport and history synchronization SHALL remain outside the preflight operation.

The sentences above hold for a carried runbook with no run check, and for a carried runbook's own records. A carried runbook that declares a run check SHALL have it evaluated in the receiving project: it SHALL need the runbook it names there, and a finished, passed run of it recorded there, and a prerequisite absent from the receiving project SHALL leave the check unmet.

runbook-authority

A project keeps its procedures in a fourth place req-a-project-keeps-its-procedures-in-a-fourth-place

A project SHALL have a place for its runbooks, beneath the project root, distinct from its accepted specifications, its active changes, and its archive. A runbook SHALL be identified by a directory in that place, and the directory's name SHALL be the runbook's name.

A procedure that is run repeatedly SHALL NOT be represented as a change. A change is completed and archived once; a procedure is run again, so a representation that archives it after its first run destroys the thing being kept.

The place SHALL be created when a project is bootstrapped, rather than when a project's first runbook is created. A location that does not exist until something is in it is not a location a contributor can find, and the bootstrap operation is already required to produce the project's places rather than to acquire them on use.

The runbook's name SHALL NOT be recorded anywhere but in its directory name, except that a run check's declaration names another runbook as data to address it, and nothing SHALL derive an ordering, a version, or a history from that name. A runbook is renamed by moving its directory, exactly as a change is. This is narrower than the prohibition on deriving a version from a directory name and it is not in tension with it: a version is a value a gate compares, so reading one out of a display string depends on a convention nothing enforces, while a name is how a thing is addressed rather than something compared to anything.

A runbook's documents SHALL reference the project's one asset store, as every other document in the project does. A runbook SHALL NOT hold a store of its own.

The project enumerates the runbooks it holds req-the-project-enumerates-the-runbooks-it-holds

The operation that lists what a project holds SHALL be able to list its runbooks, as a selection alongside its changes and its accepted specifications. A separate top-level command SHALL NOT be introduced for it, because listing what the project holds is an operation that already exists and a second command would invent a second vocabulary for it.

The enumeration SHALL be derived from what the place holds rather than from a list a contributor maintains. A maintained list is the shape this project refuses when it forbids a gate from reading the last archived version out of a directory name: a value kept beside the facts it summarizes depends on a convention nothing enforces.

Each entry SHALL carry what a selector needs in order to choose between runbooks, rather than a name alone: its version, its title, and the documents it holds. A runbook whose procedure is absent is the case that matters, because a name alone cannot distinguish it from one that is complete.

The enumeration SHALL name a runbook once, whatever number of documents it holds. An enumeration that listed each document separately would present one runbook as two entries.

The operation that starts a run, named with no runbook, SHALL list the project's runbooks as this enumeration does, say how to start one, and refuse as an invocation missing its argument does. A runbook's name and title SHALL reach a terminal with each character a terminal would not show as itself written as its escape, and the structured listing SHALL carry them as they are.

Each entry SHALL also carry whether the runbook's declarations can be read, its prerequisites, and whether every one is met. Each prerequisite SHALL carry the requirement declaring it, the runbook it names, its lowest release where declared, its state, one of met, unmet, missing, self, cycle, or unavailable, up to three records that satisfy it with a count of all, and the names of its runs still open, whether met or not. Only a met or unmet prerequisite SHALL name records, and a runbook whose declarations cannot be read SHALL NOT read as met. The enumeration SHALL name one cycle for each group of runbooks whose run checks lead back to one another, the shortest through the group's least runbook.

Asked to, the enumeration SHALL draw the same order as a Mermaid flowchart, with one arrow from each prerequisite to the runbook requiring it, styled met, unmet, or cycle, in the same bytes on every machine. A name SHALL reach the drawing escaped, so no name breaks the diagram or acts as a Mermaid directive. The drawing asked for without the runbook selection SHALL be refused as a usage error, and the structured listing SHALL be unchanged by it.

runbook-execution

A run's conformance to its runbook's contract is reported and never gated req-a-run-s-conformance-to-its-runbook-s-contract-is-reported-and-never-gated

A run whose record does not attest to every requirement the runbook states SHALL be reported, and the report SHALL name both the run and each requirement nothing in it attests to. A report naming only the run leaves a reader to find which requirement, and a report naming only the requirement leaves them to find which run.

The report SHALL NOT fail validation and SHALL NOT be promotable to a failure. A run under way legitimately has requirements nothing has verified yet, because a run is one record from start to finish, so a check that failed the run halfway through would gate the work that produces the very records it asks for. This is the same policy that keeps a specification in progress from failing: the report marks work for attention rather than blocking it.

The subject of the report SHALL be a runbook and its runs together, so the check SHALL be evaluated where both are in view rather than as part of validating one document. Neither document alone can decide it: the specification states the requirements and the record states the outcomes. A check that ran over each document would report one violation once per document, and none of it would be a fault of the document being validated.

The report SHALL NOT be produced for:

  • a runbook nobody has run. A procedure with no runs violates nothing, and a report firing from the moment a runbook is created is one a reader learns to ignore.

  • a run whose recorded outcome states that the check did not pass. The obligation is that the run recorded an outcome, not that the outcome was good. A batch that came out wrong is a bad batch and a correct record of one, and a report that treated a recorded failure as a document fault would teach an operator to record a pass.

  • a run attesting to something the runbook does not require. A run that recorded more than it owed is not at fault.

Whether a record satisfies another runbook's run check SHALL be a separate question, answered from its task states and recorded outcomes. A record that does not satisfy one SHALL NOT thereby be at fault, and the conformance report SHALL stay advisory and never gate the run it describes.

toolchain-packaging

The command surface checks a runbook's machine prerequisites req-the-command-surface-checks-a-runbook-s-machine-prerequisites

The command htmlspec preflight <runbook> SHALL report whether the selected runbook can start in the invocation's working environment. It SHALL support the existing --store and --json selectors. Store selection SHALL locate the runbook and its records, while machine requirements SHALL be evaluated against the invocation's working directory and environment, not the store's directory. Names SHALL follow the existing runbook-name rules.

The report SHALL identify the runbook, carried version, source location, working directory, aggregate readiness, and each checked requirement with its expected condition, observation, outcome, and remedy for a refusal. Results SHALL follow document order. Human output SHALL lead with readiness. JSON output SHALL contain one structured report with no probe output interleaved.

Where a check's declaration carries a remedy, the JSON report SHALL carry it as runbookRemedy on that check whatever its outcome, and the human report SHALL show it, labelled as the runbook's words, after the program's own remedy for a check whose outcome is unmet, unavailable, timed-out, or probe-failed, and never for one that passed. A declaration's field name, tool name, and path, a runbook's name, and a record's name SHALL reach a report with each character a terminal would not show as itself named by its code point, and the probe SHALL use them as written. The help of preflight and run SHALL point to the operation that lists the project's runbooks.

A completed preflight with unmet requirements SHALL exit 1, a successful one SHALL exit 0, an invalid invocation, invalid contract, or operational failure SHALL exit 2, and cancellation SHALL exit 3. Unknown observations such as an unavailable manifest environment or a timed-out probe SHALL be reported as unmet readiness rather than as success. An invalid declaration SHALL remain distinguishable from a valid check whose condition is false.

htmlspec run <runbook> --as <record> SHALL apply the same checks and exit distinctions before creating a run. htmlspec run <runbook> --check SHALL continue to report existing run conformance without checking the present machine. Existing JSON fields on successful run creation SHALL be preserved, with version and preflight information added.

With --json, a run check SHALL carry a run object in preflight and run alike: the runbook it names, its lowest release, its state, one of met, unmet, missing, or unavailable, the records that satisfy it and how many, each record that does not with its reason, and its runs still open. A report that is not ready SHALL still name a met run check's open runs. A runbook named on the command line SHALL be known by one of the project's runbook directory names: the typed name when it is one; else, among the names for the same directory, the one equal to it ignoring case; else the directory's only name. Only a name that stays ambiguous SHALL be refused. The report, the source location, and the record SHALL carry the name it resolves to.

Evidence

A backup, then a migration, in a scratch project evidence-a-backup-then-a-migration

On 2026-10-27, in a scratch project where migrate declares preflight/v3 {"kind":"run","runbook":"backup"}, htmlspec run migrate --as first-migration exited 1 while the only backup run, nightly, had 1 of 2 steps done, naming nightly as still open, and created nothing. Once nightly was finished and passed, the run started, and its record's verification read "1 finished, passed run(s) of backup meet it: nightly (0.1.0)".

What the change settled beside its contract rows evidence-what-this-change-settled-beside-its-rows

Four behaviours have no row of their own and are stated where they fall: the unavailable state, which replaced the draft's unknown before release; reading a record's release from its head alone; the escaping of runbook and record names in every report; and the resolution of a typed runbook name, which keeps every name 1.1.1 ran. A fifth reaches beyond this change's requirements: a parse recovery's message quoted the character the parser stopped at raw, and now names each character a terminal would not show as itself by its code point, through the rule htmlspec-parse's visible module holds, so validate --json gives a different message for such a document with the same diagnostic code.
