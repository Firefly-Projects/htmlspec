Record how a risk was settled

This change adds one requirement to document-schema, "A risk records how it was settled": a risk declares in a data-response attribute whether the change mitigated it or accepted it, a risk that declares no response is mitigated, and only a mitigated risk must state a mitigation. It modifies no accepted requirement, because none stated a risk's parts, which only the schema and projection section 4.7 required.

document-schema

A risk records how it was settled req-a-risk-records-how-it-was-settled

A risk unit SHALL contain a heading and one statement of how the change can fail. It SHALL declare how the risk was settled, in a data-response attribute with one of the values mitigated or accepted. A risk with no data-response attribute SHALL be interpreted as mitigated.

A mitigated risk is one the change holds down. It SHALL state what holds it down, in a paragraph marked data-field="mitigation". Where the mitigation does not reach all the way, that paragraph SHALL say how far it reaches. The extent SHALL NOT be a value of its own.

An accepted risk is one the change bears as it stands. It SHALL NOT be required to state a mitigation. It MAY carry a mitigation paragraph, which then states the part of the risk that something does hold down. It SHALL NOT be required to state its reason in a field of its own, because its statement and its notes are where an author says why a risk is borne.

The reason is that a risk is settled in one of two ways, and only one of them has a mitigation to state. A contract that required a mitigation of every risk left the author of an accepted risk two choices: invent a mitigation, or write a mitigation paragraph that denies being one. Either choice puts a false statement in the risk's mitigation.

The default is mitigated because every risk written before this attribute existed had to state a mitigation. Each such risk keeps both its meaning and its validity.

The response SHALL be stated separately from the unit's change state. The change state says what a change does to the risk, and the response says how the risk is answered. Neither SHALL be inferred from the other. The response SHALL NOT be inferred from whether a mitigation paragraph is present, because an accepted risk may carry one.

The response SHALL project into the risk's response field, which every risk in the model carries, including a risk whose markup declares none. A data-response attribute on a unit other than a risk SHALL project nowhere, as a data-state attribute does on a unit other than a task.

The following SHALL make a document invalid:

  • a risk whose response is mitigated, declared or by default, with no mitigation paragraph;

  • a data-response value other than mitigated or accepted.

A mitigated risk

When
a risk carries data-response="mitigated", a statement, and a data-field="mitigation" paragraph
Then
the document is valid, and the risk's response is mitigated

A risk written before the response attribute existed

When
a risk carries a statement and a mitigation paragraph, and no data-response attribute
Then
the document is valid, and the risk's response is mitigated

A mitigated risk with no mitigation

When
a risk whose response is mitigated, declared or by default, carries no mitigation paragraph
Then
the document is invalid, and the report names data-response="accepted" as the declaration for a risk the change bears

An accepted risk with no mitigation

When
a risk carries data-response="accepted" and a statement, and no mitigation paragraph
Then
the document is valid, its response is accepted, and it has no mitigation

An accepted risk that names a partial measure

When
a risk carries data-response="accepted", a statement, and a mitigation paragraph
Then
the document is valid, its response is accepted, and its mitigation projects as it would on a mitigated risk

A risk that is partly held down

When
a risk carries data-response="partial"
Then
the document is invalid, because how far a mitigation reaches is stated in its paragraph and not in the response

A response on another unit

When
a decision carries data-response="accepted"
Then
the attribute projects nowhere, and the decision means what it would mean without it

How a risk is settled today, measured against HEAD evidence-the-measurement-2026-09-30

Measured on 2026-09-30 with htmlspec 0.18.0, before this change (evidence/the-measurement-2026-09-30.md). The release runbook's two risks were the repository's only risk units outside the fixtures, the template, and archived changes, and one held a mitigation paragraph whose first sentence says the risk is not mitigated. A risk with a statement and no mitigation failed validate with schema/invalid, "mitigation" is a required property, and adding the attribute, then named data-disposition, changed neither the diagnostic nor the model, so the only valid accepted risk was a mitigation that says it is not one. A per-unit attribute on the wrong unit was ignored silently. All 83 projected risks in tracked HTML and all 11 in the staged migration carried a mitigation. The 24 archived designs held 88 risk entries, 15 marked accepted, 21 mitigated, and 3 not mitigated, and 12 headed risks, and five risks were accepted and named a partial measure. Three migration readers met the gap, and shape kept 93 of the 100 risk statements out of units, while the missing value blocked 5.

What the implementation revealed evidence-what-the-implementation-revealed-2026-10-08
