Separate artifact kinds

This change replaced the lifecycle status a document declared with a declared artifact kind, one of a closed set of four, and made a change a directory of kinded documents (A document declares its identity and provenance, A change is a directory of kinded documents). It separated each kind's intended content, which guides an author, from its enforced structure, which alone decides validity: the proposal and the design are unconstrained, a task unit in a specification and a tasks document with no task unit are errors, a specification requirement that no verification names is reported as a warning, and the contract rules apply to every kind (Intended content is guidance and enforced structure is validity, A proposal argues value and the tool does not shape it, A design reasons in whatever form the reasoning takes, A tasks document carries the workable items, A specification states the settled contract as built, A specification must not carry task units, A tasks document must carry at least one task, A specification requirement without verification is reported as incomplete, The contract rules apply to every kind). It had initialization take a kind and create a whole change, and made archival an agent's production of the change's specification, which validation checks before the agent applies its contract to the accepted specifications (A single command exposes validate, format, and init, Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven, Archival produces the specification and then updates the accepted contract, Archival produces the specification and updates the accepted contract).

artifact-kinds

A change is a directory of kinded documents req-a-change-is-a-directory-of-kinded-documents

A change SHALL be represented as a directory of separate documents rather than as one document carrying every stage. Each document SHALL declare which artifact it is, and the set of kinds SHALL be closed: proposal, design, tasks, and specification.

The separation is the point rather than a filing convention. Each kind has a different job and a different degree of formality, and a single document carrying all of them cannot hold a proposal to a looser standard than a specification.

A change SHALL NOT be required to have every kind present. A change may be proposed before it is designed, and designed before its tasks exist.

Intended content is guidance and enforced structure is validity req-intended-content-is-guidance-and-enforced-structure-is-validity

Each artifact kind SHALL have an intended content and an enforced structure, and the two SHALL be distinguishable by a reader. Intended content directs an author or an agent writing the document. Enforced structure is what validation checks, and it is the only thing that decides validity.

The canonical mapping SHALL be:

Kind Intended content Enforced structure

proposal

Value, need, scope, alternatives

None

design

Architecture and technical reasoning

None

tasks

Workable implementation items

At least one task unit

specification

Requirements, scenarios, evidence, verification

No task units; a requirement with no verification is warned

Where a kind's enforced structure is None, a document of that kind SHALL NOT be invalid for carrying any unit, nor for carrying none. For those kinds, "belongs" in the intended-content column is authoring guidance and never a validity boundary.

A unit's appearance in a kind SHALL NOT be prohibited unless this specification names that combination. The only named prohibition is a task unit in a specification.

A proposal argues value and the tool does not shape it req-a-proposal-argues-value-and-the-tool-does-not-shape-it

A proposal document SHALL be free to use any structure its author finds effective. Its job is to argue value and need, and validation SHALL NOT require any semantic unit in it.

Validation SHALL NOT report a proposal for carrying any unit either. An author who finds it clearest to state a prospective requirement while arguing for it SHALL be able to, in prose or as a unit, with no diagnostic.

The distinction is between permitted and authoritative. A requirement unit in a proposal SHALL be valid, and it SHALL NOT be treated as part of the contract: the contract is what a specification states. A consumer determining the project's obligations SHALL read specifications and SHALL NOT read a proposal's units as binding. This is what "requirements do not belong in a proposal" means, and it is a statement about authority rather than about validity.

Prose SHALL be sufficient. A document of headings, paragraphs, figures, and tables SHALL be a conforming proposal with no unit markup at all.

The contract rules that apply to every document — parseable markup, resolvable references, no remote or executable content — SHALL still apply. Freedom of structure is not freedom to ship a document that will not open or render offline.

A design reasons in whatever form the reasoning takes req-a-design-reasons-in-whatever-form-the-reasoning-takes

A design document SHALL move a proposal's argument into architecture and technical consideration, and SHALL be free to structure that however the reasoning requires.

Validation SHALL NOT require any semantic unit in a design, and SHALL NOT report a design for carrying any unit. Architectural reasoning is continuous — the connections between considerations carry as much as the considerations do — and a tool that pushed an author to fragment it into addressable units would destroy the thing being written.

A design that records its decisions and risks as units SHALL be valid, and so SHALL a design that reasons the same ground in prose and marks nothing. Which of those an author chooses SHALL be their judgment rather than the tool's.

A tasks document carries the workable items req-a-tasks-document-carries-the-workable-items

A tasks document SHALL carry at least one task unit. This is a completeness rule rather than a placement rule — it constrains what the document must contain rather than which units are permitted in it — and a tasks document carrying other units alongside its tasks SHALL NOT be reported for them.

A task SHALL be individually addressable, so that a state change is a change to one unit rather than to a list.

A specification states the settled contract as built req-a-specification-states-the-settled-contract-as-built

A specification document SHALL carry requirement, scenario, evidence, and verification units. These four are retrospective: evidence and verification can only be recorded after building, and a requirement reaches its final wording once the built shape is known.

A specification SHALL be the artifact that survives its change. It SHALL describe what the change established rather than what it intended, so a reader of the accepted contract reads what was built.

A specification SHALL NOT carry task units, and validation SHALL report one that does as an error. A task that is done is history, and the specification records the obligation the task satisfied rather than the work that satisfied it. This SHALL be the only placement rule in the vocabulary, and it is an error because a specification is read by tools and by later changes as the statement of the contract.

Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven req-every-synthesis-and-every-contract-update-is-agent-driven

Four operations move content between artifacts, and each SHALL be performed by an agent following stated rules and guides rather than by a program that parses one artifact and emits another:

  1. Creating a design, using the proposal.

  2. Creating tasks, using the proposal and the design.

  3. Producing the change's specification, from the completed change.

  4. Updating the project's accepted specifications, from that specification.

The first three are synthesis: they produce a new artifact from artifacts that remain intact and separate. A design does not absorb its proposal — it is informed by it, and both survive as their own documents. Calling these merges would misdescribe the four-document model, in which no stage is folded into another.

The fourth is a merge, and it is the only one. It applies a change's contract into the project's accepted specifications, which already exist and hold content this change did not touch.

The reason all four need an agent follows from the freedom the upstream kinds are given. A proposal and a design may be structured any way their authors chose, so there is no grammar to parse. Each operation is an act of reading: deciding what a paragraph obliges, which existing requirement a new scenario belongs under, and what to leave alone. A parser cannot do that, and one written to try produces artifacts that satisfy every check while misrepresenting their source.

The merge SHALL apply partial updates rather than replacing wholesale. Adding one scenario to an existing requirement SHALL be expressible without restating the requirement, and content the merge does not mention SHALL be preserved.

The project SHALL supply the rules and guides that direct these merges, as instructions rather than as code. What the project SHALL implement in code is the checking: validation reads the merged artifact and reports what is wrong with it. That division is the whole arrangement — judgment where judgment is needed, mechanical checking where a rule is exact.

Archival produces the specification and then updates the accepted contract req-archival-produces-the-specification-and-then-updates-the-accepted-contract

Two documents are in play at archival and there SHALL be exactly two: the change's specification artifact, which lives in the change directory, and the project's accepted specifications, which live outside it and hold the contract for the whole project. The change's specification and its "as-built specification" SHALL be the same file. A change directory SHALL hold at most one specification artifact.

Archival SHALL proceed in this order:

  1. The agent creates the change's specification artifact, or updates it if the change already has one, from that change's proposal, design, and tasks.

  2. Validation checks it. Archival SHALL NOT proceed past this point while it reports an error.

  3. The agent applies that specification's contract changes to the project's accepted specifications.

  4. The change directory is archived intact, with all four of its documents preserved.

The change's documents SHALL survive archival. A reader SHALL be able to open an archived change and find the argument that motivated it, the reasoning that shaped it, the work that delivered it, and the contract it established — which is the whole reason for keeping the stages separate.

The specification SHALL be produced by an agent following stated instructions rather than by a deterministic extractor.

The reason is that the mapping is judgment rather than transformation, and the freedom the upstream kinds are given is what makes it so. A proposal and a design may be structured any way their authors chose, so no rule can locate the obligation inside them. Deciding which paragraph states one, what its identifier is, and which scenarios express it is reading rather than parsing. An extractor that guessed would emit documents that satisfy every check and misrepresent their source, which is worse than producing nothing.

The produced specification SHALL be validated, and the agent SHALL reconcile the diagnostics it reports. This is the division the process depends on: the agent supplies the judgment, and validation checks the artifact.

Production SHALL be idempotent in effect. Running it twice against an unchanged change SHALL NOT alter the specification's meaning.

document-template

A document declares its identity and provenance req-a-document-declares-its-identity-and-provenance

Every document SHALL declare, in machine-readable form, the schema version it conforms to, the change or spec it represents, and its lifecycle statuswhich artifact kind it is. A reviewer SHALL be able to see this information on the page without reading source.

The declared kind replaces the per-document lifecycle status. A stage — exploring, proposed, accepted, implementing, verified, archived — is a property of a change as it moves, not of each file in it: a proposal is a proposal for its whole life, and a specification does not become a proposal again. Declaring a stage per document therefore recorded the wrong thing about the wrong subject, and nothing in validation ever read it.

A document SHALL NOT declare a lifecycle status. Where a reviewer needs to know how far a change has progressed, the presence of its documents reports it: a change with a specification has been built, and a change with only a proposal has not.

document-validation

A specification must not carry task units req-a-specification-must-not-carry-task-units

Validation SHALL reject a specification document carrying a task unit. The report SHALL name the task and say that a specification records the obligation a task satisfied rather than the work that satisfied it.

This SHALL be the only placement rule in the vocabulary. No other combination of unit and kind SHALL be prohibited, and validation SHALL report nothing about any other. In particular, a requirement unit in a proposal, a task unit in a design, and a decision unit in a tasks document SHALL all be accepted without diagnostic.

The rule is an error because a specification is read by tools and by later changes as the statement of the contract, and a task appearing there would offer work as though it were an obligation.

A tasks document must carry at least one task req-a-tasks-document-must-carry-at-least-one-task

Validation SHALL reject a tasks document carrying no task unit. The stage exists to enumerate work, and a tasks document that enumerates none records nothing.

This is a completeness rule and not a placement rule. It constrains what the document must contain rather than which units are permitted in it, so a tasks document carrying other units alongside its tasks SHALL NOT be reported for them.

A specification requirement without verification is reported as incomplete req-a-specification-requirement-without-verification-is-reported-as-incomplete

Validation SHALL report a requirement in a specification document that no verification names. A specification is an as-built record, and a requirement with nothing attesting to it records an intention rather than a result.

This SHALL be advisory — a warning, never an error. Verification arrives as work completes, so a specification in progress legitimately has requirements not yet verified, and a rule that failed the document would gate the work that produces the verification.

A requirement that a verification names SHALL NOT be reported. This rule SHALL apply only to the specification kind: a requirement unit appearing in any other kind SHALL NOT be checked for verification, because it is not part of the contract there.

The contract rules apply to every kind req-the-contract-rules-apply-to-every-kind

The rules that make a document readable, parseable, and offline SHALL apply to all four kinds without relaxation. Freedom of structure at the proposal and design kinds SHALL NOT extend to markup that cannot be parsed unambiguously, a reference that resolves to nothing, a remote or executable dependency, or an asset reference that disagrees with the bytes beside it.

A kind decides how much shape validation requires. It SHALL NOT decide whether a document opens.

toolchain-packaging

A single command exposes validate, format, and init req-a-single-command-exposes-validate-format-and-init

The project SHALL provide one command-line program offering, at minimum, a subcommand to validate documents, a subcommand to format documents, and a subcommand to initialize a new document or project. Each subcommand SHALL be usable without configuration in a conforming project.

Initialization SHALL take the artifact kind of the document to create, and SHALL produce a document already shaped for that kind. A proposal SHALL be initialized as a page of prose with no unit scaffolding at all, and a specification SHALL be initialized with the units its kind expects.

The asymmetry is the requirement. A proposal that arrived full of empty requirement scaffolding would push its author toward stating a contract at the stage meant for arguing about one, and the emptiest possible starting point is the correct one for a document whose structure is the author's to choose.

Initialization SHALL be able to create a whole change — the directory and its documents — rather than one document at a time, because a change is a directory of kinded documents.

Archival produces the specification and updates the accepted contract req-archival-produces-the-specification-and-updates-the-accepted-contract

The command surface SHALL provide an archival operation that produces a change's specification artifact from its proposal, design, and tasks, validates it, and then applies its contract changes to the project's accepted specifications.

There SHALL be one specification artifact per change, and it SHALL be the same file as that change's as-built record. The project's accepted specifications SHALL be a separate destination outside the change directory.

The production SHALL be performed by an agent following stated instructions rather than by a deterministic extractor in the program. The program's part SHALL be the checking: it validates the produced document and reports what is wrong with it. That division is deliberate — the judgment of what a design's prose obliges is not derivable by rule, while whether the result conforms is exactly what a validator determines.

The project SHALL NOT ship a command that parses a proposal or a design and emits requirements. Those kinds carry no imposed structure, so there is nothing for such a command to parse reliably, and one written anyway would produce a specification that passes every check and misstates what the change established.

Archival SHALL NOT complete while the produced specification reports an error. A change archived with an invalid specification would leave the accepted contract in a state the project's own tools reject.
