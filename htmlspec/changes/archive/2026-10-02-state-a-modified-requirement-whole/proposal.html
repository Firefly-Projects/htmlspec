Why

Nothing in a change's specification can tell a withdrawn scenario from one that was merely not restated. artifact-kinds' "Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven" says:

The merge SHALL apply partial updates rather than replacing wholesale. Adding one scenario to an existing requirement SHALL be expressible without restating the requirement, and content the merge does not mention SHALL be preserved.

So a change may state part of a requirement it modifies. A scenario it leaves out may be withdrawn, or it may be kept. The text is the same in both cases, and only the merging agent's reading decides.

This project's own archive shows the cost. evidence/the-measurement-2026-09-30.md compares every requirement block under ## MODIFIED Requirements in the archive, 34 of them across 19 changes, with the accepted specification at the commit that merged it:

  • 31 restate the requirement whole.

  • 3 leave out a scenario the accepted specification then held, and they point opposite ways.

    • 2026-08-14-separate-artifact-kinds left "Status visible to a reviewer" out of document-template's "A document declares its identity and provenance", and its merge withdrew the scenario.
    • The same change left "Unknown subcommand" and "Discoverable help" out of toolchain-packaging's "A single command exposes validate, format, and init", and its merge kept both.
    • 2026-08-21-distinguish-command-names-from-skill-names left out "One identity per workflow" to withdraw it. It said so in its proposal and tasks, and nowhere in the delta.

The partial rule came from OpenSpec, which states two rules here. Its spec-driven schema says a MODIFIED block "MUST include full updated content" and warns that "Using MODIFIED with partial content loses detail at archive time". The openspec-sync-specs workflow installed in this repository (generated by OpenSpec 1.7.0) says the opposite: "To add a scenario, just include that scenario under MODIFIED - don't copy existing scenarios". separate-artifact-kinds quoted that workflow's "you can apply partial updates" in its proposal and wrote the rule into the contract.

OpenSpec has since moved. The OpenSpec on this machine is 1.13.1. Its sync workflow now says "A MODIFIED block carries the whole requirement", and its validate and archive refuse all three of the partial blocks above, including the two that were meant as withdrawals.

The partial rule also blocks the change after this one. see-a-modification-the-merge-did-not-take compares a modified requirement's text with the accepted text. A partial restatement always differs from the accepted text, so that comparison cannot mean anything while the partial rule stands.

Why now. The project's migration out of OpenSpec is staged and waiting. Its staged specification for separate-artifact-kinds carries "Unknown subcommand" and "Discoverable help" only as a note, because no rule says whether a modified requirement must state them. Without this change the gap would be recorded there as an open question, or worked around in the migrated documents.

What Changes

  • A change's specification states a modified requirement whole. That means every statement and every scenario the requirement will hold once merged. Each scenario carries its own change state and the identifier its accepted counterpart carries.
  • A withdrawn scenario is stated, not omitted. It is a unit marked removed, which document-schema's "Semantic units declare a change state" already provides, and the merge deletes it.
  • An accepted scenario left out is a defect, not a reading. The merging agent reports it to the contributor, and neither keeps it nor deletes it. Each default reading was wrong for at least one of the three recorded cases.
  • The merge applies a modified requirement as stated, and preserves every requirement the change does not state. The partial-updates paragraph in "Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven" is replaced, and so is the outcome of its scenario "Merging a scenario into an existing requirement". The paragraph's last clause, that content the merge does not mention is preserved, is kept, because it is about the other requirements.
  • OpenSpec's full-restatement rule is chosen on purpose. The partial rule is recorded as rejected in the requirement and in the design, with its reason.
  • The merge instructions say so. sync.md and archive.md stop telling an agent to apply partial updates, and say how to state a modified requirement and how to merge one.
  • Unchanged: the document contract. No unit, field, attribute, or projection rule is added, so CONTRACT_VERSION stays 0.3.0, the version reject-change-markup-inside-a-unit set. No validation rule is added, and no command changes behavior. Whether a restatement is whole can only be checked against the accepted requirement, and that comparison is see-a-modification-the-merge-did-not-take's. Two requirements already agree with the rule and stay as they are: "A specification states the settled contract as built", and "A rename reaches the accepted contract as a new heading on the same identifier".

Capabilities

New Capabilities

None.

Modified Capabilities

  • artifact-kinds:

    • Added: "A change's specification states a modified requirement whole". It is added rather than folded into "Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven". That requirement is about who performs the four operations: an agent, and not a parser. This rule is about what a change's specification must contain. The partial clause sat there as merge advice while it actually governed what a specification may omit, and that is part of why its conflict with OpenSpec's schema went unnoticed. A requirement of its own also gives the next change a heading to cite.
    • Modified: "Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven". The partial-updates paragraph becomes two rules: the merge preserves every requirement the change does not state, and it applies a modified requirement as stated. The scenario "Merging a scenario into an existing requirement" keeps its name and gets the new outcome. OpenSpec 1.13.1 refuses a MODIFIED block that drops a scenario name, and the same example with a different answer is the clearest record of the reversal.
    • Checked and unchanged. "A specification states the settled contract as built" asks that a reader of an archived change's specification can determine its obligations without the other documents, and a whole statement is what makes that true of a modified requirement. "A rename reaches the accepted contract as a new heading on the same identifier" says "Where the change also modified the requirement, the merge SHALL apply the modification to the same accepted requirement". That sentence now means applying it as stated, and needs no edit.

Impact

  • No code changes. The rule is enforced by the agent that writes and merges a specification, under the instructions below. The checking arrives with see-a-modification-the-merge-did-not-take.

  • crates/htmlspec-assets/instructions/sync.md:

    • "Apply partial updates." (lines 105-109) is replaced.
    • "Never copy a change's specification into an accepted specification wholesale." (111-114) stays, and says it is about the document, not a modified requirement.
    • "Merging a rename", step 4 (134-136), says "as a partial update like any other", and changes.
    • A new section on merging a modified requirement. The procedure today works only through absent and untakenRenames, and a modified requirement is in neither, because its identifier is already held.

  • crates/htmlspec-assets/instructions/archive.md:

    • Step 2 gains how to state a modified requirement. Today it covers only a renamed one (162-185).
    • Step 5's "Apply partial updates. Preserve what you do not mention." is replaced, with its quotation of OpenSpec's sync guidance and its first and third bullets (260-273).

  • Checked, and not edited:

    • new.md, continue.md, and ff.md each tell an agent not to write the specification.
    • apply.md, verify.md, and explore.md describe neither a specification's contents nor the merge.
    • migrate.md has no ## MODIFIED Requirements row. See "Not in this change".
    • assets/template-specification.html: its one example requirement is added, and it says nothing about modifying one.
  • Installed copies, regenerated with init and never edited: .claude/commands/htmlspec/{sync,archive}.md, .claude/skills/htmlspec-{sync,archive}-change/SKILL.md, .codex/skills/htmlspec-{sync,archive}-change/SKILL.md, .pi/prompts/htmlspec-{sync,archive}.md, and .pi/skills/htmlspec-{sync,archive}-change/SKILL.md.

  • Tests:

    • crates/htmlspec-assets/src/instructions.rs gains a test that no workflow tells an agent to apply partial updates, and that sync and archive name the new requirement.
    • A conformance fixture, valid-modified-requirement-stated-whole, pins that a modified requirement holding unchanged, modified, added, and removed scenarios is valid. It already is, and the fixture keeps it so.

  • Not in this change, and recorded so it is not assumed to follow:

    • A check that a modified requirement is stated whole. It needs the accepted requirement, which one document does not hold. see-a-modification-the-merge-did-not-take compares a change's modified requirements with the accepted text in sync and archival. No validation rule is added here.
    • Marking which words of a statement changed. That is mark-the-words-a-modification-changed. A diff view is show-what-a-modification-changed.
    • How migration completes a source MODIFIED block that left a scenario out. migrate.md has no row for ## MODIFIED Requirements. Completing a block from the git record is an obligation for project-migration, and this change does not add it. For 33 of the 34 modified requirements, the staged migration already states every scenario the accepted specification then held as a unit. The 34th is the separate-artifact-kinds case, which carries its two kept scenarios in a note. Under this rule they become unchanged units.

    • This repository's installed OpenSpec skills (.claude/skills/openspec-sync-specs/SKILL.md and .claude/commands/opsx/sync.md, both from OpenSpec 1.7.0). They still carry the partial rule. They are OpenSpec's generated files, not the product's, so regenerating or removing them is a decision about this repository's tooling, not about the contract.
