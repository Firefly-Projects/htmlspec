Mark the words a modification changed

HTML's change markup has one meaning in the projected model, under "A deleted word is recorded apart from what a unit states": the text of an ins stays in the field around it, and the content of a del supplies no field and is recorded in the deletions of the requirement or scenario it belongs to. Under "Change markup in or around a semantic unit is rejected", validation permits the marks only inside a requirement or a scenario marked modified in a change's specification, never in a heading or a renamed-from paragraph, and rejects them in or around every other unit. Under "A marked modification reaches the accepted contract as its current text", applying a change's contract gives the accepted unit its current text and leaves no change markup in the accepted specification.

document-schema

A deleted word is recorded apart from what a unit states req-a-deleted-word-is-recorded-apart-from-what-a-unit-states

HTML's change markup SHALL have one meaning in the projected model, wherever a document uses it. ins marks text an edit added, and its text SHALL contribute to the field that encloses it, as the text of any other inline element does. del marks text an edit removed, and its content SHALL contribute to no field of the model. A paragraph inside a del SHALL supply no statement, note, or other field. A description-list entry inside one SHALL supply no scenario entry. A heading inside one SHALL supply no title. A link inside one SHALL project no reference. The link SHALL still project into the document's links, because the page still carries it and a reader can still follow it.

The text of each del SHALL be recorded in the deletions of the requirement or scenario it belongs to, one entry for each del that no other del encloses, in document order. A del belongs to the nearest unit that encloses it, so a deletion inside a scenario is the scenario's and not its requirement's. A del whose own class names a unit is that unit: it projects from its own content, records no deletion, and is recorded in the deletions of no unit around it. A del in any other unit, or outside every unit, SHALL be recorded nowhere. The document's summary, a figure's caption, and a link's text SHALL hold only the current text.

Migration note: the delta stated: "The text of each del SHALL be recorded in the deletions of the requirement or scenario it belongs to, one entry for each del that no other del encloses, in document order. A del belongs to the nearest unit that encloses it, so a deletion inside a scenario is the scenario's and not its requirement's. A del in any other unit, or outside every unit, SHALL be recorded nowhere. The document's summary, a figure's caption, and a link's text SHALL hold only the current text." Read by those words, a del whose own class names a unit, nested in a requirement, is recorded in the requirement's deletions. As built, it is that unit: it projects from its own content, records no deletion, and no unit around it records it. Task 3.3 stated and pinned the case, and task 10.1 took and fixed the review's finding that a nested <del class="scenario"> was recorded in its requirement's deletions, which evidence/mutation-testing-2026-10-06.md also records. The sentence above on such an element is the build's; the accepted specification carries the delta's words.

The reason is that a unit's statements are the contract, and the contract is the text as it now reads. Read as presentational markup, a deleted word ran into the statement beside the words that replaced it: a statement read "its active changes, and its archive, and its runbooks", which is neither the text before the edit nor the text after it. Keeping deleted words out of every field makes the normative text always the current text. Recording them keeps in the model what the page shows a reader, which is that the unit deleted words, and which words. A requirement and a scenario record them because an accepted specification holds those two units and no others, so they are the units whose accepted text a change edits.

The record is a list of deleted texts for each unit, and not one for each field. A deleted paragraph belongs to no field, and a deleted phrase's place in its sentence is in the markup, where a reader sees it. So the model does not pair a deletion with the insertion beside it.

Inserted text SHALL NOT be recorded a second time. It is current text, and it is already in its field.

The projection SHALL apply this whatever the document is and wherever it sits. The projection decides what the markup means, and validation decides where the markup is permitted, under document-validation's "Change markup in or around a semantic unit is rejected". So a document that validation rejects for its markup still projects with its deleted words out of every field.

A unit inside a del SHALL still project as a unit. A unit is created by its reserved class, and a withdrawn unit says so with its change state. Validation rejects the del around it.

document-validation

Change markup in or around a semantic unit is rejected req-change-markup-in-or-around-a-semantic-unit-is-rejected

Validation SHALL reject a document in which an ins or a del element sits inside a semantic unit, encloses one, or is one because its own class names the unit, except in the one place this requirement permits it. These two elements are HTML's change markup: del marks text an edit removed, and ins marks text an edit added. An element of the same name inside SVG or MathML belongs to that language and is not change markup. Each such element SHALL be reported as an error under a rule identifier of its own. The diagnostic SHALL name the element and the unit it sits in or encloses, and SHALL quote the element's text, so that an author can find the edit in a long statement. It SHALL also say why the markup is not permitted where it sits.

Change markup SHALL be permitted inside a requirement or a scenario marked modified, in a change's specification. There, del marks words the change deleted from the accepted text and ins marks words it added, and document-schema's "A deleted word is recorded apart from what a unit states" keeps the deleted words out of every statement and entry. A change's specification is the one document whose modified units restate accepted text as the change edited it, for the merge to apply. So it is the one place where "these words are new" and "these words were deleted" are claims about something a reader can check: the accepted text before the merge. The markup is permitted there, not required. A modified unit that marks no words is valid, because whether its marks are complete can only be checked against the accepted text, which one document does not hold.

The permission SHALL be decided by the unit the markup's text belongs to, which is the nearest unit that encloses it. So a scenario marked modified inside a requirement marked modified may carry markup. A scenario marked unchanged inside that requirement may not, because it states that its text is the accepted text.

The permission SHALL NOT extend to any of the following, in a change's specification or anywhere else:

  • a unit marked added, removed, or unchanged. Every word of a new unit is new, so there is no accepted text to mark against. A removed unit is withdrawn whole, and its change state says so. An unchanged unit states that none of its text changed;

  • a decision, evidence, risk, or verification unit, whatever its change state. An accepted specification holds requirements and scenarios, so no other unit has accepted text that a change's marks could be read against;

  • a task, in any document. A task gives up its words by being dropped with a reason, and its state already shows that;

  • markup in a unit's heading, or in the paragraph stating the heading a requirement replaced. A heading is a name and not a statement. A requirement's new name is stated with a renamed-from paragraph, which the merge and its comparison already read, and a scenario keeps its identifier when its heading changes;

  • markup that encloses a unit, or is one because its own class names the unit, because a unit's change state already says whether it is new or withdrawn.

A change's specification SHALL be recognized by where the document sits: the specification directly within a change's directory, whether the change is active or archived. A change's proposal, design, and tasks, an accepted specification, a runbook's documents, a file beneath a change's evidence, and a document outside any project SHALL NOT carry the permission. A document SHALL NOT be able to claim the permission by what it declares, because a change's specification and an accepted specification declare the same kind. A document outside any project sits in no change, so the rule applies to it without the permission.

The reason is that the projection gives change markup no meaning. An element that carries no reserved class is presentational, and its text contributes to whichever field encloses it. So a deleted phrase in a requirement's statement becomes part of the normative statement, run together with the words inserted beside it. A deleted paragraph becomes a second statement. A deleted link still counts as a reference. A unit wrapped whole in del projects as a unit the document still states. In each case the author reached for the right HTML and wrote a wrong contract, and nothing reported it.

The reason for the rule is that change markup states a history, and a unit has a history to state only where it restates accepted text as a change edited it. Anywhere else the markup says something false or empty. An accepted specification that kept a mark would go on claiming that a word is new. An unchanged scenario with a mark would claim both that its text is the accepted text and that part of it is not. A new unit would mark as new some of the words that are all new. Before document-schema's "A deleted word is recorded apart from what a unit states" gave del its meaning, the markup also corrupted the model. A deleted phrase ran into the statement beside the words that replaced it, a deleted paragraph became a second statement, and a deleted link still counted as a reference.

It SHALL be an error and not a warning. A warning reports a document that is incomplete or needs review, and it never gates work. Here the model is wrong: it states, as current, words the author withdrew. A document whose model says what its author did not mean does not conform, and a report that let it through would be read as saying it does. Markup outside its one place is neither. The document states something false about the contract, and a document whose markup contradicts its own units does not conform. This rule is also the check that a merge left no markup in an accepted specification. As a warning, it would let every check a project can run accept an accepted contract that still shows a deleted phrase on its page.

It SHALL cover ins as well as del, and permit them in the same place. They are one vocabulary for one edit. An inserted word does no harm to the model, because its text is current text and the projection keeps it. It is rejected outside the permitted place anyway, because a unit that marks one side of an edit and not the other states half of a diff. A reader sees words marked as new and cannot see what they replaced. word marked as new states an edit, and no edit of accepted text is being stated there. Inside the permitted place, an insertion with nothing marked as removed is a whole edit: a change that only adds a word to a list deletes nothing.

The rule SHALL apply to every unit in every kind, and whatever contract version the document declares. A document SHALL NOT exempt itself from the rule by declaring an earlier version, because a rule a document can switch off protects nothing.

Change markup outside every unit SHALL NOT be reported by this rule. A unit is where a document states its contract, and prose outside every unit projects into no unit's field. A document MAY mark a wording change with del and ins in that prose, as a proposal showing a before and an after would.

An element that only draws a line through text, such as s or the obsolete strike, SHALL NOT be reported by this rule. HTML defines s as content that is no longer accurate or relevant, not as an edit to the document, and it stays presentational.

artifact-kinds

A marked modification reaches the accepted contract as its current text req-a-marked-modification-reaches-the-accepted-contract-as-its-current-text

Where a change's specification marks words in a requirement or a scenario it modifies, applying that specification's contract to the project's accepted specifications SHALL give the accepted unit its current text: the content of each ins element without the element, and nothing of each del element.

The accepted specification SHALL NOT carry change markup. The accepted contract states what the project requires now. Which words a change added and which it deleted is history, and it belongs to the archived change that made the edit, whose specification keeps its marks. This is the arrangement "A rename reaches the accepted contract as a new heading on the same identifier" makes for a heading, where the accepted specification does not carry the renamed-from paragraph.

A mark carried into the accepted contract would go on claiming that a word is new for as long as the text stands. This project's history holds the case. 2026-09-18-establish-a-runbook bolded the words it added to two project-bootstrap requirements, the merge copied the bold into the accepted specification, and it is still there, where it now reads as emphasis. The word the same edit deleted was never marked at all. A del carried across would show the next change's author a deleted phrase on the page of the contract, ready to be copied back as current.

Validation holds the second half of this: an accepted specification that carries change markup in a unit is rejected, under document-validation's "Change markup in or around a semantic unit is rejected". The first half is the merge, which remains the agent's work under "Every synthesis and every contract update is agent-driven". This requirement states what a correctly applied modification leaves behind, not a program that applies it.

The current text the merge writes is the text the change's specification projects. document-schema's "A deleted word is recorded apart from what a unit states" keeps deleted words out of every statement and entry, and keeps inserted words in. So a comparison of a change's modified requirement with the accepted one, made on projected text, finds a merge that wrote the current text equal to the change. It finds a merge that kept a deleted word, or left out an inserted one, unequal. The marks need no reading of their own to be compared.
