Requirements

Machine requirements are explicit and mechanically checkable req-machine-requirements-are-explicit-and-mechanically-checkable

A runbook SHALL state machine prerequisites as ordinary requirement units in its specification, with stable identifiers, normative statements, scenarios, and an explicit check declaration. Preflight SHALL read these declarations from the existing projected requirement notes. It SHALL NOT infer checks from prose, requirement names, or executable examples. The declaration format SHALL be documented and restricted to built-in checks; arbitrary commands SHALL NOT be accepted.

The supported needs SHALL include a tool at a stated version, a tool supplied by a pinned package-manager environment, an installed agent skill, and a working-directory state. Machine requirements SHALL remain part of the logging contract: passing preflight does not remove their obligation to have an outcome recorded. Ordinary requirements without a check declaration SHALL remain logging obligations without becoming machine checks.

Malformed declarations, unknown check kinds, duplicate declarations on one requirement, and unsupported format versions SHALL be reported against the requirement and SHALL prevent starting a run. A contract with no machine declarations SHALL report zero checks, without claiming to have established unstated prerequisites.

A second declaration format, preflight/v2, SHALL read every form preflight/v1 reads and one optional field, remedy: text saying where to get what the check needs. A remedy SHALL be one line of 1 to 200 characters once the spaces around it are trimmed, holding no control character, no whitespace but the plain space, and no invisible character, Unicode's format and default-ignorable characters, beyond the two joiners and one emoji presentation selector directly after the character it is for. A remedy breaking any of these SHALL make the declaration malformed. A remedy is text: preflight SHALL NOT run it, open an address in it, or count it as satisfying the check. preflight/v1 SHALL keep refusing the field, and an unsupported format version SHALL be reported naming the formats the program reads.

A third declaration format, preflight/v3, SHALL read every form preflight/v2 reads, with its remedy, and one more kind, run, whose fields are runbook and an optional atLeast, and whose need is a finished, passed run of that runbook recorded in the same project. preflight/v1 and preflight/v2 SHALL refuse run as an unknown kind, and the fault SHALL say it is declared in preflight/v3. A run check that names its own runbook, and one whose run checks lead back to its own runbook through others, SHALL be malformed, and SHALL prevent starting a run as every malformed declaration does, naming the cycle in order.

A prerequisite run is met by a finished, passed record req-a-prerequisite-run-is-met-by-a-finished-passed-record

A run check SHALL name its runbook by a runbook directory whose name equals the declared name byte for byte, whatever the filesystem. A name that no runbook directory holds SHALL leave the check unmet, with a remedy that says to add the runbook or correct the name.

A record under that runbook's runs/ folder SHALL satisfy the check when it parses without error recovery, is a tasks document, has at least one task done and every task done or dropped, and records an outcome of passed for every requirement it carries. Any satisfying record SHALL meet the check, because a record states no time and no record is the latest. A record that is a symbolic link, or resolves outside that runbook's runs/ folder, SHALL NOT satisfy it, because the run it records may have happened in another project.

Where atLeast is declared, a record SHALL satisfy the check only when the runbook release it states is at or above atLeast within the same major. A record's release SHALL be read from the one htmlspec-runbook-release meta in its head and from nowhere else, and a record that states none, states one that is not three dot-separated integers, or states more than one SHALL NOT satisfy a check with atLeast.

A record that does not satisfy the check SHALL be named with the first reason it does not, in this order: it is a link, it cannot be read, it needs error recovery, it is not a tasks document, it has no task done, it is still open, it recorded an outcome other than passed, it records no outcome for a requirement, or its release is missing, malformed, stated twice, or misses atLeast. Up to three satisfying records SHALL be named, with a count of the rest, and every run of the named runbook still open SHALL be named whether the check is met or not.

A specification reached through the run checks that cannot be read, needs error recovery, or does not validate SHALL leave the check unavailable, naming that runbook, and never met.

Package manifests retain authority over package pins req-package-manifests-retain-authority-over-package-pins

A requirement for a package-managed toolchain SHALL identify the existing manifest, selected environment, and tools needed from it rather than repeat the manifest's versions in the runbook. Preflight SHALL delegate interpretation of pins to that package manager's supported read-only adapter and check the tools the invocation would actually use against that environment.

The initial supported package-manager adapter SHALL cover a local locked Nix flake's development shell. Preflight SHALL NOT install, build, download, update locks, enter a shell, or alter the working environment. Missing cached dependencies, an unsupported manifest, an unavailable manager, or an inability to establish a tool's provenance SHALL refuse readiness with an explanation. The existence of a manifest or a shell marker alone SHALL NOT count as evidence of readiness.

Preflight gates the creation of a run req-preflight-gates-the-creation-of-a-run

Starting a run SHALL perform preflight against the selected runbook and the invocation's current working directory and environment before creating the record or its directory. A standalone preflight success SHALL NOT be reusable as permission to skip this check. Only a result in which every declared machine requirement passes SHALL permit record creation; failed, unknown, timed-out, and unsupported results SHALL refuse it. There SHALL be no force or skip-preflight option.

Preflight SHALL execute no procedure steps and SHALL not modify the runbook, existing records, skills, or target working tree. Checks SHALL have bounded execution and captured output. An interrupted preflight SHALL stop its probes and publish no record. Each retry SHALL collect fresh observations. Readiness describes observations at the beginning of a run, not a guarantee about the environment throughout the procedure.

A successfully created record SHALL carry the runbook version and a verification of each passed machine requirement, with its observation and method. Other logging requirements SHALL remain unverified until the run records their outcomes. Checking or editing an existing record SHALL NOT become subject to preflight; run conformance remains advisory as established by log-a-runbook-run.

A run check SHALL read only the records of the runbook it names, in the project the declaring runbook is read from, the store's under --store, and SHALL read nothing else. An unmet run check SHALL refuse the start as an unmet machine requirement does, before anything is written, and no option SHALL override it. A new record SHALL state the release of the runbook it ran in an htmlspec-runbook-release meta in its head, and the verification it carries for a passed run check SHALL name up to three satisfying records with their releases and a count of the rest.

A carried runbook does not require its source project's history req-a-carried-runbook-does-not-require-its-source-project-s-history

A carried runbook SHALL be usable with its specification, procedure, version, changelog, and the referenced shared asset store in a minimal receiving project layout. It SHALL NOT require the source project's archive, project declaration, accepted specifications, or old run records to perform preflight or start a new local run. The receiving layout SHALL preserve one asset store shared by the runbooks and their records; it SHALL NOT introduce a store inside each runbook.

The runbook SHALL require tools and skills from the receiving machine rather than vendor them. Its dependency manifests SHALL be supplied by the target work environment when required. Old run records SHALL remain at their source unless separately copied; their absence at the destination SHALL NOT imply they never existed or prevent a new run. Transport and history synchronization SHALL remain outside the preflight operation.

The sentences above hold for a carried runbook with no run check, and for a carried runbook's own records. A carried runbook that declares a run check SHALL have it evaluated in the receiving project: it SHALL need the runbook it names there, and a finished, passed run of it recorded there, and a prerequisite absent from the receiving project SHALL leave the check unmet.
